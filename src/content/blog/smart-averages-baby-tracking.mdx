You kept a careful log on Monday. On Wednesday, grandma helped and nobody opened the app. By Friday, you have a week of entries with a few gaps and a question: what does your **baby feeding average per day** actually describe?

It describes the information you recorded. It cannot tell you what happened during the gaps. Nubo Intelligence brings statistics and useful context to that record, without treating an unlogged feed as proof that your baby did not eat.

## Recorded totals and actual care are different

Imagine this illustrative week:

| Day | Bottles recorded | What you remember |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | 6 | You logged throughout the day |
| Tuesday | 5 | You logged throughout the day |
| Wednesday | 0 | A caregiver handled feeds without logging |
| Thursday | 2 | You remember missing entries |
| Friday | 3 | Some feeds were not recorded |
| Saturday | 5 | You logged throughout the day |
| Sunday | 4 | You know there were more feeds |

The log contains 25 bottles. Dividing by seven gives about 3.6 **recorded bottles** per day. That arithmetic does not establish your baby's actual intake, and the app cannot recover the missing amounts from the table.

The distinction matters when you share a record. Tell your pediatrician which days you logged and where you know entries are missing. A total is more useful when the person reading it knows its limits.

## How Nubo Intelligence adds context

Nubo Intelligence includes daily totals, counts, durations, averages, typical ranges, and comparisons. Different views answer different questions: a total shows what you recorded for a day; a typical range summarizes a supported pattern across recorded days.

Statistical checks look for enough observations and a sufficiently consistent pattern before showing a typical range. The calculations limit the influence of a single unusual high day. They do not certify that a day is complete or fill in care that was never entered.

This is why a typical range may be unavailable even when some entries exist. A few scattered records can be useful history without being enough to support a broader estimate. Missing records remain unknown.
