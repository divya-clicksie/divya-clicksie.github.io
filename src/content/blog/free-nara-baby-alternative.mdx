If you are looking for a **free Nara Baby alternative**, you may be trying to solve two problems at once. You need a place to log the next feed, nap, and diaper without adding another bill. You also do not want months of careful entries to disappear when you change apps.

Nara has been a useful tracker for many families. Its move to paid tracking does not erase what people like about it. The question is whether its new plan still fits your household, or whether an app with an ongoing free tier now makes more sense.

## What changed with Nara Baby?

[Nara's current FAQ](https://nara.com/pages/nara-baby-app-faqs) describes a 7-day trial. After that trial, a family needs a monthly subscription or lifetime purchase to add new tracking entries. Without payment, existing entries remain available to view and export. Nara says a single payment covers the whole app family, so caregivers do **not** each need a separate subscription.

Those details matter. "Paid" does not mean your history vanishes, and "family plan" does not mean a charge for every grandparent. It does mean that ongoing logging is no longer an indefinitely free option under the plan described in Nara's FAQ. If you already use Nara, check the notice in your own app for when the change applies to your family.

## Decide what you actually need to keep free

Think about the last week, not a feature list. Which events did your household log every day? For many families it is feeds, sleep, diapers, and the occasional medicine or note. If a partner or grandparent takes a shift, they need to see the same timeline and add their own entries. That is the everyday work Nubo keeps in its free app.

If you depend on a dedicated pumping-output record, exports, or advanced views, compare paid options honestly. Nubo Plus is optional for routine baby tracking, but those specific tools are not part of the free tier. If you want to avoid a subscription altogether, [our checklist for a baby tracker without a subscription](/blog/tracking/free-baby-tracker-no-paywalls/) separates essential logging from paid extras.

The logging method matters too. A notebook is still free and reliable when one person is tracking at home. A phone app is easier when several caregivers need one timeline. A physical button can help when the baby is in your arms and your phone is out of reach. Nubo offers an optional device for those moments, but you do not need to buy it to use the free app. For a wider view of those trade-offs, see [paper, app, and physical baby tracking compared](/blog/tracking/physical-baby-tracker-vs-app/).

## What to do with your Nara history

Before you switch, export a copy of your data. Nara's FAQ says to open the main Activity screen, tap the avatar beside your child's name, and choose **Export Data**. The export is a CSV file. Nara also says you can export after the app becomes read-only, so there is no need to rush through this while holding a baby.

Save that file somewhere only your family can access, then open it to make sure it contains the history you expected. A CSV is useful as an archive or for a spreadsheet. It is not automatically a transferable baby timeline. Nubo's public site does not document a Nara CSV importer, so do not assume your old entries will appear in Nubo with one tap.

You can still make a clean handoff. Keep the Nara export for past history. Set up your baby and caregivers in the new app. Add the current details that affect the next shift, such as the last feed, active sleep timer, recent medicine, and a short handoff note. You do not have to recreate every diaper from three months ago to know what happened today.

If Nara still fits your family, staying is a reasonable choice. If you want ongoing everyday tracking without a required subscription, [explore the free Nubo app](/app/) and see whether its routine feels right before moving your household over.
